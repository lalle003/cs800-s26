# Dissertation Chosen

Unfolding Particle Detector Effects and solving QCD Inverse Problem with Generative AI by Tareq Alghamdi

Particle physics experiments at facilities like Jefferson Lab rely on detectors to capture the products of high-energy collisions. However, these detectors are imperfect, they blur measurements through smearing, miss entire regions of phase space through acceptance gaps, and lose events through inefficiency. Recovering the true underlying physics from this distorted data is Tareq's primary problem he is solving.

Tareq addresses this problem using generative AI. He deveolopeed a GAN framework that learned to simulate the detectorm what it would generate, then reverse the problem to get the true solution.

Building on that, he went ont to tackle the inverse problem, training a GAN only on measured data and using that to predict regions hthat the detector never saw.

Finally he combined these two ideas, and used them to solve the QCD inverse problem.

In this presentation I will walk through the two published papers that form the foundation of his work, explain how the dissertation builds on and connects them, and show how the full pipeline from raw detector data all the way to fundamental QCD parameters represents a meaningful step toward modernising data analysis in high-energy physics.
